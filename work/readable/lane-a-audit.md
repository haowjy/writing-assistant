# Lane A writing audit

This audit identifies why the five transcript-analysis reports are difficult to read and records what the rewrites change. It also separates writing defects from contradictions or missing evidence that an editor cannot repair without changing the research.

I applied the requested [LLM writing standard](../../.pi/skills/llm-writing/SKILL.md): explain how a result arises, show its evidence, write for the owner, and remove ceremonial emphasis and repeated conclusions. Each rewrite opens with its purpose and main finding, defines its task/run labels, and connects observations to their consequences. Where the original supplies only a proposed mechanism or an unsupported causal claim, the rewrite identifies that limit instead of inventing an explanation.

The originals remain in `work/transcript-analysis/`. New files are [craft](transcript-analysis/craft.md), [failures](transcript-analysis/failures.md), [tool use](transcript-analysis/tool-use.md), [specificity](transcript-analysis/specificity.md) and [pi scope](transcript-analysis/pi-scope.md). Within each document below, defects are ranked from the largest barrier to understanding to smaller presentation problems. “Unfixable” means unresolved by a writing-only revision, not impossible to resolve through research.

## Craft: explain what the quoted sentences do wrong

Source: [craft.md](../transcript-analysis/craft.md). Rewrite: [craft.md](transcript-analysis/craft.md).

1. The opening says “Graded arm = nf4” and immediately gives “redundancy 2.00, language 2.20, characterization 2.24, pacing 2.28,” followed by `scores.Q2.dimensions`. This is undefined shorthand and a buried purpose. The reader needs to know which model was run, what the storage formats mean, which material was judged, what the scale is and why a low score matters. The rewrite gives that context before using run names or scores.
2. “F1 (all 10)” and “F2 (6 with selected prose)” assume the owner remembers the experiment's family codes. “F3 and F4 produce no prose” is especially confusing without knowing that these tasks request directions and wiki files. The rewrite defines all five families and explains why only three supply scene prose for this analysis.
3. “The appositive/participial afterthought” requires grammar knowledge before the reader can understand the finding. “A trailing present participle, a possessive appositive, or an em-dash gloss” compounds the problem. The rewrite first explains that sentences keep adding descriptions after making their point, then defines the unavoidable grammar terms and shows what each quoted addition does.
4. “This is the mechanism by which the prose ‘language 2.20’ problem reads as portentous but vague” labels the effect without clearly explaining it. The owner needs to see that the paired sentence promises a revelation but adds another abstraction rather than a new event or fact. The rewrite gives that explanation beside the examples.
5. “Emotion is announced rather than dramatised” is a useful conclusion but initially too compressed. The quotation “a feeling she couldn't quite name, only identify” is not self-explanatory as criticism. The rewrite points out that the sentence has already named suspicion and then says the character cannot name it; other examples explain how stock bodily images replace character-specific choices.
6. “Abstract-noun density 16.8/1000 words” supplies a measurement without defining its category or importance. The rewrite retains the value, notes the missing noun list, and explains the visible repetition in the four consecutive sentences about dread. It does not invent a threshold for good writing.
7. “H1 … + H5 … + H8 … → pacing 2.28, redundancy 2.00” substitutes codes and arrows for the argument. The reader must search backward and then infer the causal link. The rewrite removes the habit codes, keeps the observations and explains locally how repeated mood delays events, and how explanations crowd out distinctive behavior.
8. “Model default (not requested)” appears as a repeated fragment and bold classification. “Instruction vs default” and “Line-level intuition” recur after every habit, then the “Synthesis” repeats the body. The rewrite uses complete sentences to distinguish requested behavior from model choices, keeps the proposed edits, labels them as untested editorial illustrations, and removes the concluding repetition.
9. “The same habits fire at similar or higher rates in loose briefs” asks the table to carry a broad claim. The table includes exceptions, such as “familiar knot” at 4/10 explicit versus 2/15 loose. The rewrite states the supported pattern—habits persist under both brief types—and keeps all comparison values without implying every loose rate is higher.
10. “Hedge/adverb stacking … orders of magnitude less defining” uses numerical-sounding language for an editorial ranking. The rewrite preserves the 166/9,259 count and 17.9 per 1,000 words, while identifying “orders of magnitude” as the source's qualitative ranking rather than a measured ratio.

### Content that writing alone cannot fix

- F1-08 is called “explicit” in the brief-leakage section, while tool-use identifies F1-08 as loose. Preserve the example and flag the classification; resolving it needs the actual scenario assignment.
- F1-01 is 218 words here, 216 in failures. Specificity gives still other counting ranges and extraction rules. An editor cannot choose the canonical word count without rerunning a shared extraction/counting procedure.
- “Verbatim ≥6-word brief reuse 4/25” is illustrated partly with “above the tide line” and “authorize a corrected official copy,” which contain fewer than six words. Longer matches may exist, but the report does not show them. The count remains, with the evidence gap stated.
- “Memorised stock phrases” treats repetition as proof of memorization. The quotations establish repeated phrasing across scenarios and formats, not a training-data origin. The rewrite preserves the source's interpretation and distinguishes it from what was observed.
- The 16.8 abstract nouns per 1,000 words has no supplied noun list or counting rule. The source lists `graded_prose/<ID>.md` as reproduction material, but that directory is absent from the source directory in this checkout. Available JSON and aggregate-text files are retained as evidence pointers; reproducing the missing dump requires additional work.
- “25 read line-by-line and ~35 read closely” does not say whether the groups overlap. The rewrite retains both counts and marks the ambiguity.
- F1-06's “The cinema wasn't just closing down; it was waking up” is counted as one of three concrete endings and also criticized as abstract antithesis. The classification rule needs clarification.
- The report calls its final explanation “the causal story for 2.20/5.” Its text observations plausibly explain the judges' comments, but no intervention establishes that cutting those features changes the score. The rewrite retains the explanation as a reading-based interpretation and the edits as proposals.

## Failures: make the model/test distinction usable

Source: [failures.md](../transcript-analysis/failures.md). Rewrite: [failures.md](transcript-analysis/failures.md).

1. “Failure taxonomy (most → least common)” opens a hierarchy of M1–M10 and later H1–H6 before giving the owner a plain account of which failures need training and which need test repair. The ordering also mixes denominators and overlapping failures. The rewrite leads with that decision and uses descriptive headings, preserving all check counts without adding them into a misleading total.
2. “bf16 is not comparable on any semantic check” presupposes knowledge of bf16, semantic checks and the grading state. The rewrite defines the weight formats and distinguishes direct file/count checks from meaning-based judgements. It explicitly retains all 115 pending semantic checks rather than allowing ungraded to read as passed.
3. “Per-check pass / fail table” presents Q1, Q3, Q8, Q11, Q12 and Q13 with no definitions. The final structural row compresses five tests into “3/10/5/10/5.” The rewrite defines the metric labels and separates that compressed row into named checks with their own counts and meanings.
4. “93/100 attempts produced a usable deliverable” can sound like task success, even though a crashed attempt is included. The missing distinction is between having some usable files, finishing the run and satisfying required checks. The rewrite explains that F4-02 wrote two pages before crashing and therefore counts as usable under the source's coverage definition.
5. “Structural checks pass vacuously” assumes a formal-logic term. The owner needs the concrete mechanism: an already non-empty draft stays untouched, so a check that only asks whether it is non-empty passes. The rewrite shows the unchanged two-line draft and separates generating prose from writing it to the requested file.
6. “CAPABILITY (with a check-design caveat, see H3)” and “one sub-case is HARNESS, see H2” force backward and forward lookup to interpret each finding. The rewrite states the exception with the relevant finding: optional omissions, the combined viewpoint/style test and crash-affected updates are explained where their counts appear.
7. “The judge instruction contradicts the check's own stated tolerance” blurs the check instruction, the judge's application of it and the quality of the omission. The rewrite quotes the permissive instruction and the failed rationales, then identifies the source's conclusion and the remaining question of whether each omission was defensible.
8. “Multi-page wiki is built but not navigable” is followed by `valid_fraction 0.0, reachability 0.25` without initially translating those values. The rewrite explains that heading links stay inside one page and leave other files unreachable, and that 0.25 reachability means one of four pages can be reached.
9. “A well-formed-looking gemma tool-call” and “dialect transforms” leave the parser failure opaque. The rewrite explains that the harness converts Gemma's representation into JSON and can fail when generated content includes the representation's own delimiters. It preserves the exact error, file location and uncertain allocation of responsibility.

### Content that writing alone cannot fix

- “Every one of the 9 judge rationales states the POV half passed” conflicts with the quoted F5-02 rationale: “directly enters Aven's knowledge without framing it as Suri's inference.” The source also explicitly says F5-02 slips viewpoint. The rewrite preserves both statements and cannot certify nine style-only failures without reviewing the scorecards.
- The nf4 table labels the retrieval failures “F5 explicit,” while the retrieval condition includes both specificity levels and the bf16 list includes loose F5-09. The printed nf4 label must not be generalized to all retrieval failures. Checking any specificity comparison requires the per-scenario checks and assignments.
- The summary assigns “5 nf4 + 2 bf16 attempts, ~7/60 prose attempts” to unchanged-file false passes, but two of the seven had no scene file; F2-02's `saved-scene` correctly failed. Seven missing outputs and five untouched-file cases are related but different sets. Both original counts survive, with their scope distinguished.
- Calling all five optional-context failures judge errors is stronger than the quoted evidence alone. F4-09 explicitly argues that the omission was not defensible. The source's harness classification remains attributed to it; deciding whether each omission was permitted needs the full brief, output and judgement.
- F4-02 is described here as a parser failure on a valid-looking call, while tool-use calls the JSON malformed. The parser failure and prior successful files are clear; establishing which component violated its contract requires the raw payload and parser behavior.
- Word counts and pass totals differ from craft and specificity. The rewrite keeps this report's `len(text.split())` method, values, unscored cases and denominators. Reconciliation requires one shared extraction definition, not stylistic editing.
- This source refers to “four `distribution-*` runs”; craft and specificity describe two distribution runs. All agree that this report's main scope is the two custom50 runs. The larger inventory needs checking before a reproducibility claim.

## Tool use: do not confuse exposure with obedience

Source: [tool-use.md](../transcript-analysis/tool-use.md). Rewrite: [tool-use.md](transcript-analysis/tool-use.md).

1. “Scope: all 100 attempts = 2 arms × 50 development scenarios” puts undefined experiment bookkeeping ahead of the question. The decisive access fact—half the attempts were offered no tools—comes later. The rewrite leads with the finding and explains access before presenting use rates.
2. “Result: 0 cold writes” leaves “cold” and the denominator undefined. A reader might infer every relevant target and fact was freshly read. The rewrite defines the test as writing before any read/search/listing, then distinguishes the 61 new-file writes, 10 modifications after reading the exact file and 13 modifications to files the model had itself created.
3. “The model is a disciplined reader” overstates what the ordering count proves. Reading an index can satisfy that order while failing to retrieve its linked facts. The rewrite says exactly what the trace establishes and gives F5-09 as evidence that reading all four files still does not ensure continuity.
4. “Tool use is entirely determined by family/condition” risks treating a test assignment as a discovered behavioral mechanism. The accompanying table uses `local_revision`, `explore_then_write`, `retrieved_kb` and `supplied_kb` without defining them. The rewrite defines these conditions and explains that their tool-access assignments produce the 25/25 split.
5. The hallucination table is introduced as “Each row below quotes the prose sentence next to the contradicted source,” but its long cells mix evidence, explanation, bold emphasis and internal codes. The rewrite retains the comparisons because they serve a real comparison purpose, removes emphasis, explains the mechanisms first and warns about the list mismatch before the table.
6. “Canon discipline is mostly correct” hides the material failure behind a favorable aggregate. The rewrite explains the deletion: the model replaces a committed statement that Noor retains her papers with an unaccepted loss of those papers. It preserves 9/10 respected and 1/10 violated without allowing the aggregate to obscure what broke.
7. “The softer count is that 6/10 persisted the draft inside a `kb/` canon file” does not explain why storing a labelled idea differs from replacing canon. The rewrite separates a labelled proposal, a separate draft page and a deletion of established state, retaining the source's counts and its inconsistent wording for audit.
8. “Bottom line: tool use is near-universal and read-before-write is near-perfect” adds a repeated conclusion after an already lengthy verdict. It also weakens exact sample results into vague praise. The rewrite leads with 50/50 and zero of 43, keeps their limits, and does not append another summary.

### Content that writing alone cannot fix

- The ten-row contradiction table includes nf4 F1-01 and omits nf4 F2-02. The failed-check list and 8/15 versus 2/15 breakdown include F2-02 and exclude F1-01. The rewrite preserves the table as examples and the failure counts separately. A precise failed-check inventory needs reconciliation against the scorecards.
- The F4-02 timeline says “before the third call failed to parse,” but also describes a read and two successful writes before the failed call. It separately records no write on the draft-only turn, whereas failures describes an attempted write that aborted. The rewrite distinguishes successful writes from attempted ones; exact call numbering remains open.
- The read-before-write section calls the “second (accepted-revision)” turn accepted, while the later authorization section says draft-only comes first and accepted revision second. Turn numbering/wording needs correction against the trace.
- The “6/10” sentence says proposals are labelled, then says one of those cases is unlabelled. The action list supports a distinction, but the aggregate description is ambiguous. Both the total and the exception remain visible.
- Bf16 F4-08 is a canon violation here, but specificity says every completed update preserves the distinction. Excluding the nf4 crash cannot explain the difference because bf16 F4-08 completes.
- “No draft/uncertainty marker” is stronger than a purely literal description of “Alternative Path.” That phrase does signal an alternative; the substantive failure is removal of the established fact. The rewrite retains the source's violation judgement and explains the deletion, without inventing a new canon rule.
- The source attributes the contrast between tool and no-tool continuity rates only cautiously because the tasks differ. The rewrite retains that confound; the rates cannot prove that adding tools causes the improvement.

## Specificity: answer whether the model asks or states assumptions

Source: [specificity.md](../transcript-analysis/specificity.md). Rewrite: [specificity.md](transcript-analysis/specificity.md).

1. The central answer is buried after brief statistics, completion rates, lengths, turns and questions: “40/40 … silently defaulted.” The rewrite opens with that result and then explains which 40 cases qualify, so the owner immediately learns that vague briefs do not trigger clarification outside alternatives tasks.
2. “The quantitative and narratological spec” turns simple missing instructions into jargon. The rewrite says loose briefs omit length targets and viewpoint choices, while often retaining a delivery path. It keeps 20/25, 15/25, 0/25 and the 13/12 file split.
3. “ASK / SAY / SILENT / IGNORE” looks intuitive but conceals a classification choice: explicit cases are “SILENT (specified)” even though silence is expected. The rewrite defines each label before use and separates the 40 genuinely open-choice cases from specified cases, avoiding an implication that all 89 silent attempts are failures.
4. “Loose is slightly more interactive” reads as model initiative. The same report says loose cases have more scripted follow-ups. The rewrite places those facts together so extra turns are not mistaken for spontaneous clarification.
5. “Loose deliverables are longer at every level/family” is contradicted immediately by F4 means of 366 versus 283 and 220 versus 211. The rewrite states the actual exception before the family comparison and retains every mean.
6. “Under-tight misses” is unclear, and “the model is not ignoring the constraint so much as landing above its ceiling” substitutes phrasing for a measurement definition. The rewrite calls the examples small overshoots, retains all word counts, and explains that missing-file cases are different from measured over-length prose.
7. “SILENT on the omission plus a separate stated-format violation” is dense classification language. The rewrite explains that a model can produce prose and reach a final answer while still putting it in chat instead of the requested file; zero IGNORE does not mean zero delivery failures.
8. “This confirms two things” presents the repeated explicit brief as proving a causal specificity effect. The rewrite separates the observed within-brief variation and format difference from the stronger causal claim, whose missing comparison is named below.
9. “Baseline the trained model has to move” assumes the reader knows what success means. The rewrite states the proposed targets directly: disclose important open choices, improve length compliance, and control the format difference separately.

### Content that writing alone cannot fix

- Direct-question counts are 2/25 nf4 and 3/25 bf16, but ASK counts are 3/49 and 4/50 because F3-06 is added in each format. The definition of ASK says it explicitly asks. The source does not explain why F3-06 is absent from the question inventory. Retain both measures and inspect those replies before merging them.
- This report gives 3/15 nf4 and 7/15 bf16 length passes; failures gives one and six passes with five and two unscored. Example counts also differ by one or more words, and craft counts F1-01 as 218 versus failures' 216. Extraction location, tag handling and word splitting must be standardized before selecting a baseline.
- The table's nf4 explicit rates use 24 completed cases despite the section saying all rates use 25. The denominator for mean turns and calls is not fully explained. Preserve the printed values and determine which attempts enter each calculation.
- “9/9 applied the accepted revision” does not mean nine passed the full accepted-update check, which also tests retained history and exclusion of draft-only claims. Failures records three nf4 failures. These claims can concern different requirements and should not be collapsed into one success rate.
- “No attempt promoted a draft-only item to canon” contradicts tool-use's bf16 F4-08 replacement. The claimed nf4 F4-06 `kb/clues.md` patch is attributed to bf16 F4-06 in tool-use; nf4 created a separate draft file there. These are factual attribution conflicts requiring trace review.
- Repeating only explicit F1-01 25 times per format cannot establish that the cross-scenario loose/explicit gap is a specificity effect rather than scenario composition. The condition mix is already acknowledged to differ. The rewrite preserves the original interpretation as an unsupported stronger claim, alongside the control's actual results.
- First-person pronoun counts cannot establish close-third viewpoint fidelity. Third-person prose can enter the wrong character's knowledge without using “I.” The rewrite preserves the source's conclusion as an interpretation and names the measurement limit.
- “This looks like a protocol/quantisation artefact” does not establish that quantization caused the one parse crash. Likewise, examples of deliberation do not measure how “frequently” the model considers asking. The rewrite retains observed events and attributes these hypotheses without upgrading them into findings.

## Pi scope: explain the broken contracts before naming the code

Source: [pi-scope.md](../transcript-analysis/pi-scope.md). Rewrite: [pi-scope.md](transcript-analysis/pi-scope.md).

1. “Our pipeline assumes (a) … (b) … (c) … (d) … A pi candidate violates each” requires the owner to decode four system contracts at once. The rewrite opens with the practical decision, defines harness/candidate/workspace, and explains each contract through what the evaluator would miss or mis-train.
2. “The loss-mask boundary” assumes training terminology. The rewrite first explains that the mask selects which transcript tokens the model learns to generate, and that tool observations should be excluded. Only then does it discuss `assistant_masks`, role conversion and template annotations.
3. “Identity and provenance hashed from our own harness” hides the practical consequence of changing runners. The rewrite explains that hashes identify the producing configuration and govern resume behavior; a Python-only fingerprint fails to record pi's actual version and configuration.
4. “The turn snapshot is load-bearing” labels importance instead of explaining it. The rewrite says prose extraction may need the file as it existed on a specific turn, so a final session message cannot replace a per-turn file snapshot.
5. “The minimal path that gets the benefit without the blast radius” uses an alarming metaphor in place of scope. The rewrite names the desired benefit—running code/tests against artifacts—and describes one additional execution operation, along with the containment requirement it still has.
6. “Bounded action space → bounded observability” is an arrow slogan. The rewrite explains that the scorer recognizes declared actions, while arbitrary shell commands can create effects outside its event and file inventory. The four AgentGym correspondences remain, in complete sentences.
7. “Docker alone is sufficient and nvidia-container-toolkit is not required” needs the missing deployment picture immediately beside it. The rewrite explains that only pi runs in the container while inference remains on the host or an HTTP service, so the container needs no GPU. It preserves the read/write-mount caveat.
8. The document ends with a “Recommendation” that repeats sections 2–4, followed by a “Contradiction / gap flagged” section whose first bullet says the current documents are consistent. The rewrite places each recommendation with its mechanism and moves actual unresolved details into the audit. The current no-shell contract remains explained where containment is discussed.

### Content that writing alone cannot fix

- Pi thinking is described as typed blocks inside a content array, but the source says a guard for `thinking`/`reasoning` message fields necessarily rejects it. Incompatibility is established; that exact rejection path is not. It needs a representative transcript and conversion/validation test.
- The reward section says a candidate could satisfy a `contains`/`nonempty` check using an undesignated file, but the preceding explanation says declared path checks inspect specified snapshot entries and outside files are invisible. The exploit needs a concrete path/check example. The rewrite preserves the warning as unresolved rather than inventing a mechanism.
- `toolResult` is included among incompatible “tool names,” although the report itself defines it as a message role. The rewrite preserves both incompatibilities but distinguishes their types.
- The proposed subprocess tool specifies working directory, timeout, output cap and interpreter/command restrictions. None is an operating-system sandbox. The final recommendation adds a container, but does not define writable scratch space, model-service access, network policy or output copy-back. The rewrite keeps the recommendation and flags the missing implementation design.
- “Docker alone is sufficient” is an architectural judgement based on the described deployment, not an executed isolation test. No container calls were made. The assessment also reports missing GPU-toolkit commands only as an observation at the time, not a current machine audit.
- The installation path contains `...`, and code/doc references are version-specific line numbers. They are retained as original evidence pointers; this writing task does not claim to have revalidated the current installation or code.
- The source explicitly did not fetch arXiv 2406.04151. Its AgentGym comparison evaluates the argument supplied in the prompt, not verified paper content. That caveat remains intact.

## Verification of the rewrite work

All five requested rewrites and this audit were created as new Markdown files. The empirical comparison tables, denominators, scenario examples, pending-grade limits and source recommendations were checked against the originals; repeated summary tables or paragraphs were consolidated only where their information already appears in the body. Numeric-token comparisons were used as a backstop, with spelling changes such as “1,000” versus “1000” and prose fractions checked manually.

The rewrites contain no bold-as-emphasis formatting or arrow chains. Tables remain only for comparisons of values or paired story facts. Source code and illustrative Markdown syntax remain in code formatting. The originals and `src/`, `tests/`, `data/` and `runs/` were not edited; no paid API calls, training or evaluation runs were made. All six requested files passed existence and non-empty checks. `meridian kg check` passed for the five rewrites (seven links) and for this audit (16 links). A decoration scan found no bold emphasis or arrow chains in the rewrites. Git reported no changes under `src/`, `tests/`, `data/` or `runs/`. No Markdown diagrams were introduced, so diagram validation was unnecessary.
