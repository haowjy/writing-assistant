# Add reusable settings with distinct voices and continuity constraints

Background agent p48 was asked to author 24 story worlds and explain how they broaden the existing ten. It completed both proposal files and verified their structure, coverage and uniqueness; the worlds were not evaluated with a model.

## What the worlds contain

A world is a reusable setting with two characters and facts the writing must respect. There are two new worlds in each of 12 setting families, meeting both the maximum of two and minimum of one per family. All 24 `prose_style` values are distinct, stricter than the rule allowing at most two worlds to share a style. Voices include comic, brash, warm, staccato and breathless, dryly bureaucratic, procedural and elegiac writing.

The worlds avoid archives, attics, manors and lighthouses. None reuses a character, object or setting from the existing ten, and checks found zero overlap in identifiers or names.

Every world contains five concrete `required` facts that must survive, a `belief` that is not established truth, a freely changeable `incidental` detail, and a specific `forbidden` assertion that does not contradict the required facts. An `update` and `new_state` pair describes a change and its resulting state. A `protected` sentence is embedded verbatim as the second line of `revision`, the text supplied for editing.

The rationale connects each world to its setting family, prose register, suitable task families and the particular repetitive pattern it is meant to counter. Its F1–F5 labels come from `development.py`: F1 is a reply scene, F2 a local revision or exploration followed by writing, F3 alternatives, F4 knowledge-base construction or updating, and F5 retrieval from a supplied knowledge base. A knowledge base is the collection of project reference files. This mapping addresses the request that settings adapt to different task types.

## Data structure and verification

The agent read `data/scenarios/worlds.json` in full and matched its exact shape: a bare JSON list, with the same 18 keys in every world. They are `id` and `title` for identity; `pov` and `other` for the two characters; `place` and `object`; `style` and `source`; `required`, `belief`, `optional` and `incidental` for supplied facts and context; `update` and `new_state`; and `forbidden`, `revision`, `protected` and `prose_style`. The `pov` name refers to the point-of-view character. The report does not describe `style` in enough detail to equate it with `prose_style`.

`json.load` succeeded and found 24 objects. All identifiers were unique, none overlapped the existing worlds, and no key set differed from the reference. Every required fact slot was present. The required-fact sentence counts all fell within the permitted four to six, and every protected sentence appeared inside its revision text. There were 24 distinct prose styles, with maximum reuse of one. The rationale contained 24 rows, all 24 identifiers, and exactly two worlds in each of 12 families.
