# Lane B: why these reports were difficult to understand

This audit identifies the writing failures in the balance proposal and three generation or verification reports, and links to rewrites that preserve their findings and recorded evidence. The largest problems are that the proposal delays its argument behind classifications, the wave reports announce success without identifying which specification they satisfied, and the reference-answer report mixes different kinds of checks before explaining them. Some apparent writing problems are genuine contradictions or missing evidence; those remain explicitly unresolved below.

The standard applied is [LLM Writing](../../.pi/skills/llm-writing/SKILL.md): explain mechanisms, support conclusions, preserve uncertainty, and write for the reader. Defects within each document are ranked by how strongly they block understanding, with the most consequential first. Command lines and captured outputs were preserved as historical evidence, not rerun or rewritten as new findings. Definitions were checked through read-only inspection of the referenced specificity, task-generation, scoring, and prose-measurement code.

## Balance specification

[Original](../training-data/balance-spec.md) · [Rewrite](training-data/balance-spec.md)

1. Buried argument. “## 2. The axis list” precedes “## 3. Target distributions” and the explanation that “RL, not supervised scaling, sets the train size.” The owner has to absorb dozens of properties before learning the proposed 300/60/60 pool sizes or why repeated training prompts are a problem. The rewrite begins with the size proposal and explains repeated prompts, relative rewards, and learning scoring quirks before the detailed requirements.
2. Unexplained organizing labels. “Four rings” introduces “HARD,” “COVERAGE,” “SAMPLED,” and “REPORTED.” The table gives brief definitions but makes the classification itself the organizing argument. The reader needs to know why equal coverage of every combination is impossible, which requirements block generation, and which are only measured afterward. The rewrite explains those distinctions in sentences and preserves all H1–H13, C1–C14, S1–S6, and R1–R9 requirements.
3. Undefined measurement shorthand. `12/25 bf16 pieces open with "The damp air of the archive"; self-BLEU 0.71` and “`prose.score_prose` D1/D2/D4” do not explain the number format, what the metrics compare, or which direction means repetitive writing. The rewrite defines bf16, self-BLEU, and all three metric codes. It distinguishes distance from a chosen reference from a judgment of writing quality.
4. Undefined task and score labels. The first inventory of family counts is “family `{F1:14, F2:12, F3:14, F4:8, F5:6}`”; the later rationale cites “`Q2/Q5/Q7–Q13`.” Even readers familiar with the project cannot interpret these counts without remembering a separate taxonomy. L0–L3 also appear without descriptions. The rewrite defines the five activities, the four levels, and each referenced Q metric. Checking those definitions exposed a real level-order contradiction, listed below.
5. Dense mechanism compressed into jargon. “distinct prompts to keep advantages high-entropy” and “the reward's read surface” require knowledge of both RL mathematics and the project's scoring architecture. The owner needs to know that GRPO compares several attempts on the same prompt, that tied rewards remove that relative signal, and that an absent check cannot expose a failure. The rewrite states those mechanisms directly.
6. Contradictory precision presented as an executable specification. “`tools` 180, `no_tools` 120” appears beside channel/tool counts that imply different totals. A precise-looking table hides a choice the implementation cannot satisfy. The rewrite retains both sets and names the conflict before the table; it does not invent corrected targets.
7. Conclusions whose strength exceeds the stated evidence. “marginals are independent by construction,” “cannot be gamed by our reward,” and “a 300-prompt pool caps reuse at ≈4” present guarantees without the allocation rule or analysis needed to establish them. The rewrite preserves them as claims of the proposal and identifies the missing support.
8. Unexplained source-control terms. “Split by source lineage group, never randomly” is a valuable rule, but its importance is delayed behind the label. The rewrite explains that adaptations, branches, extracted reference pages, and rephrasings can share characters and facts, making held-out scores misleading if split apart.
9. A table substitutes for an explanation of two learning methods. “SFT versus GRPO: one pool, two selection filters” assumes the reader knows what either consumes. The rewrite defines imitation training versus reward training and explains why a poor candidate response can disqualify an SFT example while its prompt remains useful for GRPO.
10. Decoration replaces priority-setting prose. “train ≫ val ≫ new test” and the bold “TRAIN : VAL : TEST” need decoding before the reader can connect the proposed spend to scarce source groups and prompt reuse. The rewrite states that training gets priority, followed by validation and then new tests, while keeping the exact ratio.
11. Ambiguous quantification. “coverage floors halved (genre ≥ 1, style ≥ 3)” and “genre / style / trope / situation | ≥ 2 / ≥ 2 / ≥ 1 / ≥ 1” look unambiguous until the reader tries to calculate a pool. The rewrite does not turn them into a false single rule; their arithmetic and scope problems remain visible.

### Content that an editorial rewrite cannot resolve

- Training tool totals conflict. The marginal target is 180 with tools / 120 without. The joint rows are file/tools 120, reply/tools 60, reply/none 30, free/tools 60, and free/none 30: 240 with tools / 60 without. The owner must choose consistent marginal and joint targets.
- The instruction-level acceptance rule contradicts the referenced code. H4 says “every L0 task has no withheld point; every L3 task with branch_choice withheld has behavior ASK_REQUIRED.” In `specificity.py`, L0 states only the always-stated applicable points and L3 states every applicable point, including branch choice. H3's level-cutoff rule agrees with the code, not H4. Decide which rule is intended; changing the prose alone would alter the specification.
- “Exactly balanced” is not the behavior of all HARD requirements. Stage and steer target rows specify 90/90/90/30, but H9/H10 assert only minimums of N // 10. H4 asserts presence rather than exact modality proportions, H12 uses floors, and H13 is a separation invariant. The statement that every axis has one ring also conflicts with later test-specific promotion of coverage properties. Decide which numbers are exact, which are minimums, and which rules differ by pool.
- Validation scaling conflicts. One-fifth of training's 120/120/60 provenance would be 24/24/12, but validation explicitly asks for human 10 / half 20 / synthetic 30. Halving the training style floor of 10 gives 5, not the stated 3. A genre floor of 3 halved also needs a rounding rule. The rewrite retains all original targets without choosing a scaling interpretation.
- Test genre scope is missing. If “≥ 2” applies to every one of 60 genres, at least 120 assignments are needed under the one-primary-genre rule, exceeding 60 test tasks. If it applies to a subset, that subset is unspecified. The same scope question affects validation breadth and how “preserve source genre” counts toward a named genre floor.
- Artifact counts lack a usable denominator. “single 180, multi 120 (among file-capable)” sums to all 300 tasks, but file-capable eligibility is not defined and no-tool cases are also required. Decide whether this counts tasks, selected outputs, or an eligible subset. A single numeric target cannot be inferred safely.
- The channel assertion is weaker than its explanation. “P(tools | reply_required) != 1” still permits zero, which is the current reply/no-tools condition the proposal says must change. The separate H7 feasible-cell floor could exclude zero, but this explicitly highlighted assertion alone does not. “P(tools | channel_free) in (0, 1)” also needs mathematical interval notation distinguished from code-style membership in a pair.
- The sampling guarantee lacks evidence. Seeded per-axis permutations can vary individual distributions, but the source does not prove joint independence or independence after compatible-ingredient replacement. The rewrite preserves the claim as unestablished rather than endorsing it as a verified property.
- The reuse and sample-size rationale contains unresolved claims. The source gives an estimated 100–200 prompt floor, an admission floor of 256, and a target of 300 without explaining the stricter minimum. Eight hundred slots over 300 prompts do not by themselves enforce a per-prompt cap of approximately four; an allocation policy is needed. “~5× the SFT seed” does not match the 19-record training seed. The rewrite retains these as different claims rather than inventing a derivation.
- The uncertainty argument needs statistical clarification. “Variance of a per-task estimate falls as 1/√n_samples” uses a standard-error-like scaling under the name variance. The claimed advantage of 12 samples on 60 tasks over one sample on 720 tasks depends on what error is being measured, including between-task variation. No variance decomposition is supplied. The rewrite identifies the problem without replacing the rationale with a new result.
- Several target semantics are missing: operational criteria for human/partly/fully synthetic provenance, the exact applicable family × level × check combinations for the 200 SFT trajectories, counts for all three clarification modalities, and how hidden or open budgets map to the fixed budget-envelope totals. Source-work floors also do not alone prevent one work from dominating; a maximum or distribution rule would be needed to guarantee that claim.
- Hash-based checks cannot by themselves establish the broad prohibition on reworded test material. The rule forbids even rephrased reuse, but the documented mechanism is content hashes. A stronger method or a narrower enforcement claim is needed. Likewise, an independently designed external benchmark is valuable, but “cannot be gamed by our reward” is an unsupported absolute guarantee.
- The original six open dependencies remain open: absent viewpoint/idea-count/ending decision points; a generator ceiling of three stages; no free-channel schema; stale schema-v2 request counts versus the accepted-task manifest and schema v3; missing emotional-register and viewpoint vocabularies; and insufficient acquired source works/groups with confirmed reuse terms. The rewrite preserves all six and the four requested decisions. It does not authorize or implement them.

## First training generation report

[Original](../training-data/wave1-train.md) · [Rewrite](training-data/wave1-train.md)

1. Success is announced without its governing scope. “All numeric balance targets are met” appears in the opening; “no numeric target was missed” returns near the end. Neither immediately says that these are the generation brief's targets, not the balance specification developed concurrently. The rewrite opens with that distinction and identifies the missing 300-task scale, four-level design, free-channel cases, provenance mix, and multiple lineage groups.
2. The table assumes the reader already knows every label. “Family | F1 14 / F2 10 / F3 6 / F4 8 / F5 10” and “REQUIRES_ASK | 12 | 12” arrive before definitions. The reader cannot know which writing activities were authored or what asking behavior is being requested. The rewrite defines families, delivery, followups, artifacts, and all three pressure labels before the counts.
3. A major missed requirement is buried as a technical disclaimer. “These are not claimed to be a balanced L0–L3 experiment” appears late in the decisions list. The owner needs this beside the opening success claim. The rewrite explains what the two-category explicit/loose split does and why it does not supply the four-level experiment.
4. Quantity looks like independence. “48 newly authored sources” sounds like broad source coverage until a later bullet says “they form one train-only lineage group.” The rewrite brings the single-group limit forward and explains why work IDs are not evidence of different authors or independent source lineages.
5. Verification jargon hides the limited conclusion. “Extraction/feasibility: 76 final artifacts extract” and “Satisfiability was reviewed” do not tell the reader that these checks use constructed outputs rather than a model solving the tasks. The rewrite explains what selectors choose, what the placeholder checks prove, and why semantic correctness or improved prose remains unmeasured.
6. The retrieval mechanism is buried under family shorthand. “F5 includes an active instruction, a withdrawn version, and twelve irrelevant documents” is strong evidence, but its role is hard to see amid the bullet list. The rewrite connects the obsolete-versus-current instruction choice to the real 183–197/1200 read-token probes and explains that reading everything exceeds the budget.
7. Count units change without enough orientation. “There are 76 artifacts because F3 selects three alternative files per case and F4 selects three KB pages per case” arrives after a 48-record table. The rewrite explicitly distinguishes task counts from per-artifact word-range counts and preserves all five ranges and the combined 520-word KB limit.

### Content that an editorial rewrite cannot resolve

- The source's pressure label is stronger than its behavior rule: “Each REQUIRES_ASK case presents a consequential unresolved branch” is followed by “The author can ask or explicitly mark a provisional assumption.” The rewrite preserves both. A research decision is needed on whether provisional action satisfies this category or whether a question is mandatory.
- Compiler success does not establish balance-specification compliance. The known gaps can be derived from the reported counts, but a comprehensive check of every new specification requirement was not performed in this report. The rewrite names known gaps without inventing a complete failure matrix.
- Literary quality, semantic distinctions, and whether a model chooses search were not measured. The report already supplies the correct limitation and `review_status: generated`; the rewrite exposes it earlier. These require later review or experiments, not editorial clarification.
- The schema rejects provenance `original`, while the generation brief preferred original/half-synthetic material. `synthetic` correctly describes the reported authorship, but it does not establish the specification's desired source mix. No spelling change can create missing human or partly synthetic sources.

## First evaluation generation report

[Original](../training-data/wave1-eval.md) · [Rewrite](training-data/wave1-eval.md)

1. The opening overstates completion through omitted scope. “All requested balance targets are satisfied. There are no outstanding blockers.” The owner cannot tell that this means a 24-task generation assignment, not the separate 60-task test specification. The rewrite states that the smaller assignment passed while the full pool, four-level design, and free-channel conditions were not delivered.
2. A test-design conclusion lacks its boundary. “This breaks the channel/tool availability confound” does not explain what changed. The rewrite states that eight reply cases now have tools while six reply cases have none, so reply no longer implies no tools; every task still prescribes delivery, so channel choice remains untested.
3. Unexplained family codes substitute for activities. Rows “Family F1” through “Family F5” and “Prose-bearing F1/F2/F5” tell the reader nothing without a remembered codebook. The rewrite defines each activity and why three families count as prose-bearing before presenting the table.
4. Major limitations are hidden inside dense authoring prose. “these are not 24 independent author groups” and “Exact ID/brief reuse checks are not proof against semantic contamination” occur after implementation detail about source records. The rewrite foregrounds the one-group limit and explains that unique identifiers cannot establish independent content.
5. Technical nouns obscure what passed. “Offline constraint witnesses establish mechanical satisfiability” means constructed example outputs can jointly meet the computable requirements. Without that explanation the reader may infer successful model behavior. The rewrite defines this evidence and retains the explicit absence of model runs, quality scores, and rubric results.
6. Labels are not separated from measured writing. “Metadata styles on loose tasks are editorial sampling labels” and the `Defaults:` check caveat are valuable but buried. The rewrite explains that metadata cannot establish output diversity and a required line cannot establish thoughtful assumptions.
7. A failed validation is hard to interpret without the mechanism. The traceback is followed by “This was a fixture error, not a task-record error.” The rewrite previews the concrete cause: the validator built a link suffix without a Markdown label; valid labeled links fixed the constructed example without weakening task constraints.

### Content that an editorial rewrite cannot resolve

- These 24 cases do not establish the full test specification. Known count/design/source-mix differences are stated; unreported properties are left unassessed. The source does not provide a complete specification compliance audit.
- One `final_eval` lineage group and zero within-catalog overlap pairs do not establish pairwise separation across all training, validation, and test pools. The report does not document a completed combined cross-pool audit. No contamination is asserted; the evidence is simply insufficient for the stronger conclusion.
- Scenario metadata uses `original`, while catalog provenance uses `synthetic`. The source explains this deliberate vocabulary difference, so it is definable rather than intrinsically unfixable; it still cannot satisfy the proposed mixed provenance target by relabeling.
- Actual literary quality, realized diversity, successful model behavior, and exclusive use of search remain unmeasured or unenforced. In particular, `evidence_exposed` accepts either read or search. The rewrite preserves this rather than implying that successful probes prove the model will search.

## Worked-reference report

[Original](../training-data/worked-examples-report.md) · [Rewrite](training-data/worked-examples-report.md)

1. The opening conflates two check inventories. “all 41 declared deterministic checks pass” is immediately followed by “There are 43 required checks: 41 have quoted author evidence and two are explicitly unmet.” The reader needs to know that mechanical passes and authored evidence about required conditions are different forms of evidence, even though both counts happen to include 41. The rewrite distinguishes them before reporting detailed coverage.
2. The most consequential caveat arrives after the coverage list. “F4-04: consequential-state and accepted-update are UNMET at turn 0.” The reader needs to know immediately why reference answers fail required conditions and whether the checks are broken. The rewrite explains the frozen antenna, the later thaw authorization, and the first-turn scope before discussing other results.
3. Unexplained identifiers conceal the task. “F5-03, F5-05, and F5-07 retrieve from files” and “F3-07 correctly has an empty list” require a family codebook and knowledge of which list is empty. The rewrite defines each family and explains that F3-07 has no required-check evidence entries because the source requires none.
4. Implementation detail precedes the meaning of verification. “creates the requested fake result around the authored response” leaves the reader unsure what was faked and what was actually executed. The rewrite explains that the response was authored, real permitted file tools were replayed, and their observations were wrapped in a simulated result for scoring. This establishes feasibility without claiming a model-generated trajectory.
5. A score needs interpretation. “valid_fraction = 1 and reachability = 1” is unexplained. The rewrite says that checked links are valid, all pages are reachable, and there are no orphan pages; it also preserves that this was extra verification for F4-09.
6. The scope is easy to misremember as final test. “the selected source scenarios retain role `development`” is buried under a later correction. The rewrite states early that these are reference answers for existing development cases, not new held-out final-test scenarios.
7. Output labels assume code knowledge. `semantic_pending=3` and `author_unmet` in the captured output are left to interpretation. The rewrite defines pending meaning-based judgments separately from the author's explicitly unmet first-turn requirements and mechanical `PASS` results.

### Content that an editorial rewrite cannot resolve

- Two required conditions are still unmet at the requested first turn. They require the antenna to turn only after a later thaw; no first-turn rewrite can honestly satisfy both the initial prohibition and those later-state labels. Full-conversation answers or explicitly stage-specific scoring would be needed, and changing either is outside this editorial task.
- Semantic assessments remain author judgments, not independent scorer results. The mechanical scorer leaves all semantic checks pending. This is missing verification, not a writing problem.
- The source supplies the aggregate inventories of 41 deterministic checks and 43 required checks, but the prose does not enumerate their complete overlap. The rewrite distinguishes them without inventing a one-to-one mapping.
- No scenario check is shown to be broken. The actual failures documented are a read-only uv cache before Python ran and an auxiliary opening audit that mistook `1.` for a shared opening. Both were fixed in the recorded run, without suppressing or relabeling example checks. The unmet antenna labels remain valid for the full conversation.
- Existing-case selection cannot supply the missing reply-with-tools condition. The rewrite retains this design gap rather than treating the 20 examples as balanced coverage of a condition absent from their source pool.

## Editorial verification

All four source documents remain unchanged. Each rewrite opens with its purpose and outcome, defines task and metric labels before relying on them, and keeps the recorded numbers, target tables, failures, commands, and caveats. The large axis catalog was converted into explanatory prose, while comparison tables and exact captured outputs remain where they carry evidence. No model, training, evaluation, or paid API call was made for this editorial work.
