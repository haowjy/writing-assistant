# SFT bootstrapping, writing RL, and reward models

Research checked 2026-09-15. The proposed direction is a short, targeted SFT warm-up
followed by RL, with direct RL from the instruction-tuned checkpoint as an ablation.
There is no evidence-based requirement to create 500–1,000 SFT targets before testing
that direction. Whether our 24-record seed is sufficient remains untested; only 19
records are assigned to train, and their synthetic content needs a provider-terms
review before acceptance. No training or judge inference ran during this research.

## Evidence from papers

| Work | Method and evidence | Limit for this project |
|---|---|---|
| [LIMA (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11206) | SFT on 1,000 curated examples, without RL, adapted a pretrained model to instruction following. | Demonstrates small-data adaptation, not sufficiency of 24 examples for writing-agent RL. |
| [DeepSeek-R1 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12948v1) | R1-Zero used RL without preliminary SFT; R1 used thousands of cold-start demonstrations and later training stages. | Primarily reasoning evidence; its cold start was not the entirety of its training data. |
| [One-shot RLVR (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20571) | Reports mathematical reasoning gains using RL with one training problem. | One unique problem still produces many sampled trajectories and updates. This is not a 24-example SFT result or a creative-writing guarantee. |
| [Writing-Zero (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00103v1) | Trains a Qwen3-32B base writer with pairwise writing rewards and BRPO, without task-specific SFT; also studies RL from an SFT writer. | The judge required substantial preparation: about 10K filtered writing preference pairs from a larger in-house collection. No-SFT writer training is not supervision-free training. |
| [RLMR (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18642v1) | Uses a writing reward model trained on human preferences, plus constraint verification and dynamically mixed GRPO rewards. | Supports separating literary quality from compliance. A verification model is not automatically an exact mechanical checker. |
| [Rewarding Creativity / RLCS (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07149v1) | Starts a story judge from over 4,000 expert preference pairs; Gemini-generated analyses filtered by label agreement and swapped order yield about 1,400 SFT examples. | [Constitutional AI (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073) | Generates critiques/revisions for SFT, collects AI pairwise preferences, trains a preference model, then uses that model during RL. | Shows how expensive AI judgments can be amortized into a reusable reward model; it studies harmlessness rather than fiction. |
| [AgentGym-RL (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08755) | Studies multi-turn agent RL across interactive environments without an SFT prerequisite. | Relevant harness pattern; it does not establish reward quality for our Markdown wikis or prose. |

Research methods and provider authorization are separate questions. A paper reporting
use of a proprietary teacher does not establish that the same use is permitted under
our account agreement, or disclose whether the authors had separate permission.

## Distinct data requirements

- **SFT demonstrations:** successful conversations and tool trajectories that establish
  behavior the current policy seldom produces. Size follows observed gaps.
- **RL tasks:** prompts, initial project files, environment budgets, and reward criteria.
  They do not each require an ideal authored answer. They still need source diversity.
- **RL rollouts:** fresh attempts generated by the current Gemma policy. Save sampled
  token probabilities, policy identity, tool observations, file snapshots, and rewards.
- **Judge data:** preference examples and/or rubrics used to establish reliable scoring.
  This is a separate collection if we train a judge.
- **Evaluation:** held-out projects and benchmarks, never recycled as optimization tasks,
  reward-model training pairs, or best-of-N training-answer selection material.

The 24 examples cannot establish broad coverage, but could help bootstrap useful
sampling on a checkpoint that already writes and calls tools. The decision criterion
is behavior: successful tool delivery on unseen training-development projects and
informative variation among sampled rewards. All-equal rewards give standard
within-group GRPO no relative reward signal; more optimizer steps alone do not fix that.

## Proposed comparison and reward design

Compare IT → RL with IT → brief SFT → RL, keeping the training task pool, rollout
budget, and rewards fixed. Retain IT and SFT-only controls. First establish a bounded
working RL batch; do not require a large SFT corpus or split planner/writer architecture
as a prerequisite. Add demonstrations where the policy rarely produces useful attempts.
This comparison is a proposal, not authorization to execute training.

Run the actual file harness for each attempt in an isolated workspace. Score final
artifacts and changes, not merely the model's claim that it completed the task.
Mechanical rewards cover required file delivery, protected edits, valid links and
reachability, and objectively checkable constraints. A semantic judge covers supported
interpretation, continuity, planning alternatives, and literary quality. A navigable
wiki can still contain poor knowledge extraction; a judge must inspect its source.

For writing rewards, extract designated prose from replies or files. Keep commentary,
KB text, and tool traces out of the prose-quality input unless needed as separate
context. Prevent missing required artifacts from earning high overall success through
attractive prose elsewhere. Do not initially optimize MMD or n-gram distance as a
per-response literary reward: retain corpus/group diagnostics and diversity checks.
Validate reward behavior on deliberately flawed cases before using it for optimization.

A judge can emit a scalar directly, compare two candidates, or generate a critique and
preference. It need not be newly trained. Possible local candidates are
[Qwen3-8B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-8B), a generative rubric judge, and
[Skywork-Reward-V2-Qwen3-8B](https://huggingface.co/Skywork/Skywork-Reward-V2-Qwen3-8B),
a scalar preference model. Both cards identify Apache-2.0 licensing. Neither has been
validated here for English literary preferences, KB interpretation, quantized scoring,
or robustness to candidate instructions. Treat them as candidates, not selected judges.
A more capable permissively licensed teacher is another option, subject to hardware.

## Compute and integration

Illustrative accounting: 1,000 prompt visits × four attempts = 4,000 rollouts.
Pointwise grading needs 4,000 judge evaluations; exhaustive pairwise comparisons need
6,000, or 12,000 when judging both presentation orders. Multiple rubric passes and
retries add work. These are examples, not planned run sizes or dollar estimates.
A local judge avoids per-token API fees but still consumes GPU time.

On the 3090, plan to test serialized generation, judging, and training phases before
assuming simultaneous model residency. Freeze the rollout policy until its batch is
scored and updated; offloading models does not justify stale-policy reuse. Measure
memory and throughput with real sequence lengths. Long tool sessions and thinking
increase sampling cost even when there is no external API bill.

[TRL's GRPO documentation](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/grpo_trainer) provides custom
rewards, tools, stateful environments, and custom rollout hooks. Installed TRL 1.13.0
contains these interfaces, verified by reading its source. Our existing SFT preparation
and evaluation runner are not yet a working RL trainer: native Gemma tool parsing,
prefix-preserving rendering, action-only loss masks, rollout probabilities, workspace
resets, and checkpoint behavior still need integration and runtime verification.

## Provider terms and existing seed

The current [OpenAI individual terms](https://openai.com/policies/row-terms-of-use/)
restrict using outputs to develop competing models. Output ownership does not remove
that restriction. The [Services Agreement](https://openai.com/policies/services-agreement/)
has specific exceptions for certain non-distributed classifiers and OpenAI model
customization; they do not establish a general permission for this Gemma project.
We have not established whether this particular use qualifies as competitive or is
covered by another agreement. Treat training authorization as unresolved.

The [Anthropic commercial terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)
and [Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms) also contain competing
model/service restrictions. Switching APIs does not automatically resolve the issue.

A benchmark report and a reward used to update model weights are different uses.
Calling the latter an evaluation does not settle its permissions. Nor is there a
blanket evaluation exception established by these pages for all account types and
uses. Retain Astra's evaluation role separately; do not assume its scores can train
our judge, select SFT targets, or drive RL. Resolve the applicable agreement for each use.

The seed includes Codex-authored material and is already pending acceptance. Keep it
as inspectable format/curation material while resolving permitted sources, replacing
training targets with eligible human material or outputs from appropriately licensed
local models where needed. This review does not mark existing records accepted or
claim that mechanically replaying a trajectory changes its original provenance.
